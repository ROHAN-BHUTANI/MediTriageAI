## 1. The Problem: Rural Telemedicine Scalability (1 min)

* **Visual**: Show the dashboard homepage or a slide mapping the rural health clinical path.
* **Speaker Script**:
  > *"Every day across India, public health platforms like eSanjeevani process hundreds of thousands of daily consultations. In rural sectors, frontline ASHA workers and community clinics act as the first line of defense, but they face a critical bottleneck: clinical triage and routing are manual, slow, and prone to human error.*
  >
  > *MediTriageAI was built to address this scale. We focus on automated clinical triage from patient symptom descriptions. Our system translates unstructured patient complaints into two immediate outputs: ESI severity classification to detect life-threatening emergencies, and specialist routing to send the patient to the correct clinical department.*
  >
  > *Our core challenge is language. Rural patients describe symptoms in Hindi, English, and code-mixed 'Hinglish'. A standard English clinical model completely fails here. We require a system that maintains perfect script-invariance."*

## 2. The Dataset & Dual-Head Architecture (1 min)

* **Visual**: Point to the "Dataset stats" section on the dashboard and the dual-head model diagram in the paper draft.
* **Speaker Script**:
  > *"To solve this, we compiled a dataset of 20,000 processed clinical notes. The text contains code-mixed Hinglish and English symptom transcriptions, mapped to 13 distinct clinical specialists (like Cardiology, Orthopedics, Pediatrics) and 5 Emergency Severity Index (ESI) tiers.*
  >
  > *Our architecture utilizes a shared multilingual encoder (mBERT or XLM-RoBERTa-large) connected to two independent classification heads. By training with a joint loss function, the model learns shared semantic representations of disease states. Crucially, we inject a custom Hinglish phonetic vocabulary into the tokenizer and align cross-script embeddings using mean anchor anchors. This ensures that 'chest pain' and 'dil me dard' map to the exact same clinical representation."*

## 4. Honest Results & Audited Limitations (1.25 min)

* **Visual**: Show the Leaderboard table and the "Known Limitation" card on the dashboard.
* **Speaker Script**:
  > *"Let us talk about our evaluation metrics, and I want to be entirely transparent. In a typical presentation, you might see a team claim 98% accuracy on severity classification and call it a day. We audited our model and discovered something critical.
  >
  > *Our classical Random Forest baseline achieved a **93.70% macro-F1** on severity triage when evaluated on the full test set. However, our feature importance audit revealed that because the ground-truth severity labels were generated by a regex heuristic, the baseline model was simply memorizing keyword triggers like 'normal limits' and 'acute distress'. This is a circular label leakage loop, not clinical reasoning. When evaluated against a clinician-annotated subset of cases, the baseline's performance collapsed by **68.48%**.*
  >
  > *To solve this, we fine-tuned state-of-the-art multilingual transformers (mBERT and DistilBERT) using real pre-trained weights to capture deep semantic clinical intent. However, our rigorous evaluation revealed a surprising outcome: these models suffered from severe catastrophic collapse on this dataset. For instance, mBERT collapsed to predicting mostly severity tier S3, achieving only a **3.0% severity macro-F1**. The models failed to discriminate between classes, highlighting a major challenge in fine-tuning deep transformers on sparse, imbalanced clinical data. Thus, in our current setting, the classical models drastically outperform the neural architectures.*"
