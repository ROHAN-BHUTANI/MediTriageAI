# MediTriageAI — Label Leakage & Feature Importance Audit

This audit evaluates whether the high performance of the TF-IDF baselines on the Severity Triage task (up to **98.25% accuracy** and **93.70% macro-F1** for Random Forest) represents genuine clinical learning or is an artifact of circular label leakage.

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Verdict

> [!CAUTION]
> **VERDICT: CIRCULAR LABEL LEAKAGE**
> The TF-IDF + Random Forest baseline's high score is primarily explained by it re-detecting the exact keyword patterns used to generate the heuristic labels. This represents a circular dependency (data leakage) rather than genuine clinical prediction.

### Overlap Statistics
* **Heuristic Keyword Overlap**: **27.00%** of the top 100 features relied upon by the Random Forest classifier for severity prediction are direct substrings or exact matches of the keywords in the regex heuristic cascade (`src/severity_heuristic.py`).
* **Feature Importance Concentration**: The top 7 most predictive features (`normal limits`, `within normal`, `in no`, `limits`, `acute distress`, `no acute`, `distress`) account for a disproportionate share of the model's decision splits, and they map directly to ESI Level 5 (`S5`) and Level 3 (`S3`) heuristic rules.

### Why Baselines "Beat" Transformers
Traditional TF-IDF classifiers easily memorize bag-of-words keywords (e.g., matching the bigram `normal limits` to class `S5`). Since the ground truth labels were generated by applying these exact keyword searches, the TF-IDF baseline achieves near-perfect performance by reverse-engineering the regex code. 

Conversely, the transformer model (e.g., XLM-RoBERTa-large, which scored **32.48% macro-F1** on the 160-sample test subset) does not have standard keyword filters and processes text semantically. Because the heuristic labels are noisy and purely keyword-based, the transformer is penalized when it generalizes beyond the exact matching strings (e.g., treating a note describing severe injury as critical, even if it lacks the exact keyword "traumatic exsanguination" required by the S1 heuristic).

## 3. Recommendations

To maintain academic integrity and prevent negative peer-reviews, we propose a two-pronged remediation strategy:

### Recommendation A: Document as a Study Limitation
* **Action**: Do not present the severity triage baseline comparison as a "headline" model success in the main results section of the paper.
* **Paper Text Modification**: Explicitly document this in the Discussion/Limitations section:
  > *"Because ESI severity labels were generated using regular-expression heuristics, bag-of-words baselines (TF-IDF) artificially achieved near-perfect F1-scores by directly memorizing key-phrases. This represents a known label leakage limitation, and these metrics should be interpreted as reverse-engineering of the rule cascade rather than generalized clinical triage capacity."*

### Recommendation B: Clinician-in-the-Loop Adjudication (Held-out Test Set)
* **Action**: Obtain clinician-annotated severity labels for a subset of the test split.
* **Execution**: Have 2-3 medical students or clinicians manually review a subset (e.g., 200 samples) and label them according to standard ESI criteria. Re-evaluate both the baseline and transformer models on this clean, heuristic-free target split to report genuine clinical generalization.
