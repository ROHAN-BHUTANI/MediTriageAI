# MediTriageAI — Bugfix Verification & Leakage Audit 

## 1. The Reported Issue
It was suspected that a results-injection bug hardcoded identical performance scores for both `mBERT` and `DistilBERT-multilingual` in `dashboard_web/data/results.json`:
- `specialist_f1` = 0.0281
- `severity_f1` = 0.1771

## 2. Extraction of Actual Metrics
I opened the raw evaluation files (`results/mbert/metrics.json` and `results/distilbert_multilingual/metrics.json`) to read the exact scores computed by the evaluation script.

**mBERT (`results/mbert/metrics.json`)**:
- `specialist_macro_f1`: 0.028114975784640544
- `severity_macro_f1`: 0.17708391413437413

**DistilBERT-multilingual (`results/distilbert_multilingual/metrics.json`)**:
- `specialist_macro_f1`: 0.028114975784640544
- `severity_macro_f1`: 0.17708391413437413

## 3. Programmatic Update
I executed `python scripts/export_dashboard_data.py` to rewrite `dashboard_web/data/results.json` directly from the raw metrics files rather than manually typing the numbers. The numbers remain identical in the final JSON.

## 4. The Verdict: Artifact or Reality?
**Verdict: The scores are genuinely identical, down to 17 decimal places. This was NOT a hardcoding bug.**

**Why? (Complete Majority-Class Collapse)**
The models did not just perform similarly; they converged to the exact same degenerate solution. The classification reports in the raw `metrics.json` files reveal that both models predicted a single class for 100% of the test samples:
- **Specialist Task**: Both models predicted Class `12` exclusively. 
- **Severity Task**: Both models predicted ESI Level `S4` exclusively.

Because both models produced constant predictions mapping to the majority classes of the test set, their confusion matrices, accuracy, and macro-F1 scores are mathematically identical. 

**Impact on Statistical Testing**
The "matched, not significant" framing ($p = 0.1931$) remains **valid and correct**. The McNemar's test and Bootstrap CI were computed on the raw predictions generated by the models (which were identically degenerate). The baseline SVM (4.82% F1) is indeed statistically indistinguishable from a model that just guesses the majority class (2.81% F1) when trained on the same constrained 3,000-sample subset without pre-trained weights.

The performance collapse is real, and the metrics reported in the paper accurately reflect this failure mode. No numbers need to be altered in the paper or dashboard.
